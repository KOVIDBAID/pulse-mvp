# Prompt-to-prototype audit

## Scope and original checkpoint

The supplied ZIP contained an attractive React prototype with 29 route identifiers, a version-2 local store, and several live actions. Many forms and cards were still static: experiment results, lab history, prescriptions, dose timelines, patient registry rows, impact metrics, and several buttons did not carry the workflow into another role.

The supplied prompt document was treated as a product specification and acceptance checklist, rather than authorization to send real messages, use real patient records, charge money or publish a site. Work was performed on the exported source. The original ZIP and prompt document were left untouched.

The delivered app completes the core hackathon journey in synthetic local state. **The complete M0–M26 product specification is larger than this MVP; the distinctions below are intentional and explicit.**

## Specification discrepancies resolved from data

- Food sprint A rises 52, 58, 49 and B rises 70, 74, 69 yield an effect of approximately **18 mg/dL**. They also meet the prompt's definition that every A rise is below every B rise. The UI uses the stated mathematical rule, rather than hard-coding “not consistent.”
- With Day 0 = 11 July 2026, Day 88 is **7 October**, and Day 90 is **9 October**. Dates derive from the simulated calendar; the original export's hard-coded report-date wording was removed.

## Operational boundary

Everything is synthetic. Stored client data and demo login credentials can be inspected by anyone using that browser. The UI privacy rules demonstrate role-specific behavior; a real deployment needs server-side authorization, authentication, secure uploads and reviewed clinical/legal operations. This delivery does not connect real messaging, payment, lab, pharmacy, insurer or AI services.
